\begin{abstract}
Context compression is increasingly proposed as a remedy for multi-turn reasoning degradation, yet when it helps remains underexplored. We investigate this question for small language models (SLMs) on multi-turn mathematical reasoning. We introduce \textit{Sparse Logic State Compression} (SLSC), a training-free twin-agent framework that maintains reasoning state as atomic mathematical propositions, and evaluate it on Math-100-multi, a 100-problem benchmark with explicit numerical dependencies. Three findings emerge: compression substantially benefits models at 4B parameters and above but yields no consistent gain at 2B or below, revealing a capability threshold around 4B; natural-language summary matches or exceeds structured SLSC in accuracy, suggesting the primary mechanism is bounded context size rather than typed representation; and formal type augmentation consistently degrades accuracy across all scales---by up to 5 percentage points---in the absence of verifier feedback. These findings clarify when compression helps and offer practical guidance for deploying SLMs in multi-turn settings.
\end{abstract}
