\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}\label{sec:overview}

SLSC encodes the accumulated multi-turn reasoning state as a sequence of atomic mathematical propositions, maintained across turns by a curator agent. Given a mathematical problem $P$ decomposed into sub-questions $q_1, \ldots, q_T$, we denote the compressed state at turn $t$ as $S_t$. The framework operates in two phases per turn $t$: (1) \textbf{Reasoning}: Agent $\mathcal{A}$ generates response $r_t = \mathcal{A}(P, S_{t-1}, q_t)$, conditioned on the compressed state from the previous turn; (2) \textbf{Curation}: Agent $\mathcal{B}$ updates the state $S_t = \mathcal{B}(S_{t-1}, r_t)$. The end-to-end inference procedure is detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:inference_alg}.

\subsection{Agent B: The Curator}\label{sec:agent_b}

Agent $\mathcal{B}$ maintains a persistent compressed state by processing $\mathcal{A}$'s response through three sequential sub-steps: extraction, optional formalization, and merging (Algorithm~\ref{alg:curation} in Appendix~\ref{app:curation_alg}).

\subsubsection{Extraction}

Given $\mathcal{A}$'s response $r_t$, the extraction step produces a set of atomic propositions $\mathcal{P}_t = \{p_1, \ldots, p_k\}$, where each $p_i$ is a self-contained mathematical claim (equation, inequality, numeric result, or named property), while ignoring procedural descriptions and section headers. A worked extraction example appears in Appendix~\ref{app:extraction_example}. Full prompt details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\subsubsection{Merging}

The merging step integrates $\mathcal{P}_t$ into $S_{t-1}$ via three LLM-prompted rules: \textbf{deduplication} (semantically equivalent propositions are consolidated), \textbf{supersession} (a new proposition that contradicts or subsumes an existing entry replaces it), and \textbf{preservation} (propositions with no conflict are retained verbatim). These rules prevent unbounded state growth while ensuring no established fact is silently overwritten.

\subsubsection{Formalization}

As an optional augmentation, each extracted proposition may be autoformalized into a Lean~4 \texttt{theorem} stub appended alongside its natural language form. Our ablation (Section~\ref{sec:lean_ablation}) shows that formalization consistently degrades accuracy; all primary results are reported without it unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{State Representation}\label{sec:state}

The state $S_t$ is rendered as a numbered list of natural language propositions, optionally paired with Lean~4 stubs, and passed directly to $\mathcal{A}$ as part of its prompt context. Each proposition is atomic; inter-proposition dependencies are not encoded explicitly. Deduplication during merging controls growth in practice. A representative $S_t$ example is shown in Appendix~\ref{app:state_example}.
